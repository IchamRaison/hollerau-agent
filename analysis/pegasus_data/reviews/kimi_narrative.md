# WHAT WE KNOW ABOUT THE BOTS (from ~150 ended practice sessions, replays attached below)

- Bots are scripted, near-deterministic: the same case + the same buyer messages give the same answers (the server even caches identical messages, so we vary subject/body). Each bot has a persona (Owner, Key Account Manager, Credit Control...), a back office whose notes we see after the end ("My call: counter / hold", "we agree"), and "reasons" in words.
- Statuses: `open` (counter), `final_offer`, `agreed` (the bot accepts one of OUR packages - it picks the package that is best for IT; e.g. P02 round 1: we sent 3 packages, it "agreed" to the cheapest price 355.93 with 14-day payment and an index clause, which for us carries a 38 kEUR relationship charge + 17.5 kEUR index-clause cost), `walked_away`. Alternatives in a bot reply (`offer:X-n-a`) cannot be accepted directly; they must be mirrored back as our own term sheet.
- Claims: cited by fact ID; the checker returns verified / contradicted / irrelevant / duplicate; max 3 per message; index windows <= 12 months. A public calibration by another team (~100 sessions) measured acceptance rates: notice-period claims 7/7, should-cost 11/14, forecast/volume 37/48, rival quotes 21/31, index 47/81, price history 4/44. Verified claims unlock concessions; contradicted ones cost credibility. After the first message, more than one new claim per round brought nothing.
- Observed bot behaviours: "we hold our Angebot" when our move is too small; `final_offer` after ~3-4 rounds; some bots freeze the price and only trade terms (P03: price frozen around 431.6, approval ladder "I must ask my management", volume focus); some demand an index clause (P02 says "no Preisgleitklausel" in the opening, then adds a CU-NOTATION clause when it agrees); some refuse chargebacks > 0 (P11), insist on firm lead times (capacity tight), refuse a cost breakdown ("our calculation is our property"), ask for faster payment ("short of cash" personas), timeouts ("just checking if you got my last email", they hold). Bots answer within seconds; our reply must come within 60 s of theirs or they time out. 
- Relationship charge (goals `stabilise` / `secure_supply`, cases P01 P02 P03 P06 P09 P10 P14 P17): the arena applies the exact formula of `arena_kit/cost.py` with a hidden supplier model. We reverse-engineered it from the cost breakdowns of our deals: **full_cost = should-cost (facts `cost:<part>`.unit_cost) / 1.02** on all three suppliers checked (ratio 0.9804, 0.9804, 0.9805) and a **supplier-specific cost of money**: 9.6 % (sup:70110, P01), 6.6 % (sup:70510, P02), 6.1 % (sup:70530, P03) - with these two parameters the charge is reproduced within 4-13 EUR on every deal. The rate is not the buyer's 8 % and we do not know yet which data field predicts it (supplier_intel has equity_ratio_pct 24.7/29.1/31.7 and credit_index 222/147/157 for those three; the bots' own price/payment trade-offs implied 6.6 % on P02 (correct) but 4.1 % on P03 (wrong)). 
- With the exact model, the U-shaped C(price) bottoms are: P01 1,096,809 (= c_best 1,096,816: any effective date, **payment 14 days**, price 128.95 Nov / 129.43 Jan / 130.65 Apr), P02 2,230,368 at 361.42 with 90-day payment and NO index clause (c_best is 2,235,514 = the bottom with 30-day payment, so the arena's c_best seems to assume the supplier's standard payment or the bot's limit), P03 3,876,431 at 418.13/90 days/24-36 months (c_best 3,909,043: the bot never went below 431.6).

# OUR AGENT (pegasus, source attached)

Code decides every number, templates write the e-mails (an LLM narrator exists but is off). Per thread: `supplier_reference` (standard terms from the quote PDF), `economics.grid/structures` (every allowed combination of payment days, effective date, contract months, lead time, index clause, incoterm, ppm, chargeback, tooling priced with the kit's cost function), relationship model (`supplier_model`: full cost 0.98 x should-cost, rate 8 %), `price_floor` = bottom of the U per structure, applied to every sheet we send (so an immediate `agreed` is never a disaster), anchor at 80 % of the ask (price-increase cases: min(92 % of ask, 94 % of current)), the structure chosen at the expected deal price (`best_at_price`), concessions in total cost C along `SCHEDULE` 0.22/0.38/0.52/0.63/... of the gap, three MESO packages per message (one lever each, farthest option, price >= 90 % of main), verified claims (ours + Codex's dossiers + a teammate's evidence adapter), an LLM reader of the supplier's reply producing typed signals with word-for-word quotes (index clause required, chargeback too high, lead time firm, price frozen, cost floor, capacity tight, cash wanted...) feeding `adapt` rules (forbidden options, required index series, `must_close` after two cost-floor statements), supplier memory across sessions (flags only after 3 observations), acceptance rules (never below the opening by 0.2 %; `final_offer` accepted when C < C_open even by 0.5 % if the alternative is worse; `agreed` accepted only when clearly below the opening; never retreat; accept when the bot's offer is at or under our anchor), RFQ cases (several suppliers): each bidder's target capped at 99.5 % of the cheapest live rival, rival cited each round, cheapest final accepted; `closing()` 90 s before the wave cap closes with the best standing offer whose C < C_open; one worker per thread, 10 threads in parallel.
